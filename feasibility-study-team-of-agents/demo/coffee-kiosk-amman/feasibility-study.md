# Feasibility Study: Specialty Coffee Takeaway Unit ("Coffee Kiosk")

Al-Jubeiha (University Street area), Amman, Jordan · 15 October 2026 · Prepared for the project owner (demo client)

> **Demo notice.** This study is a demonstration run of the feasibility agent team on a sample idea. Several parts of the brief were **assumed rather than supplied by a real client**: the target area (a mixed office/university district such as Abdoun, Sweifieh, Khalda or near the University of Jordan), the capital budget (up to about JOD 40,000, equity only, no loan) and the owner's experience (no prior café experience; an experienced barista is hired). All research is desk research dated 15 October 2026. It is not legal, tax or investment advice. Currency is the Jordanian dinar (JOD), pegged at 1 USD = 0.709 JOD.

---

## 1. Executive summary

**The idea.** A small specialty-coffee takeaway outlet selling espresso drinks, cold brew and bought-in pastries, walk-up and via Talabat/Careem, aimed at students, office workers and young professionals. The brief asked for a 15–25 m² street kiosk. The legal research found that Greater Amman Municipality (GAM) issues **no new licences for street coffee sales** [66], so the study plans a **licensable 25–45 m² shop unit with a walk-up window** on a side street in Al-Jubeiha, between the University of Jordan and Princess Sumaya University.

**Verdict: GO WITH CONDITIONS.** Demand and unit economics support the project: the gross margin is normal for coffee (about 67%) and even the risk team's deliberately cautious Prudent case returns a positive NPV. But the margin of safety is thin, and the project depends on one yes/no licensing question that must be answered before any lease is signed.

**Key numbers (JOD, 5 years, 16% discount rate)**

| Indicator | Base (owner unpaid) | **Prudent (planning case)** | Optimistic (base volume +20%) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Startup cost | 35,690 | **44,049** | 35,690 ¹ |
| NPV | 146,444 | **25,803** | 197,061 |
| IRR | 112.6% | **31.1%** | 141.9% |
| Payback | month 15 | **month 37** | month 12 |
| Year-1 net profit | 15,862 | **−15,352** | 25,774 |
| First month with positive EBITDA | 2 | **8** | not reported |

¹ The optimistic run is a volume-only sensitivity on the base model; the model output does not list its startup cost separately. Base and Optimistic are **not** recommended for planning: their 30–34% net margins are two to three times the 10–15% café benchmark [91]. The Prudent case adds an owner salary, a third barista, seasonality, volume −20%, unit cost +15% and JOD 5,000 key money.

**Top 3 risks**
1. **Licence refused for the chosen unit** (score 20/25, deal-breaker). A refusal after lease and fit-out loses about JOD 22,500, more than half the capital.
2. **Volume below plan** (16/25). Prudent NPV reaches zero at 68% of planned volume (32% below plan).
3. **Price pressure** from Qahwa BLK's JOD 2.00 latte (12/25). Prudent NPV reaches zero if the ticket falls 8.6%, to about JOD 2.24.

**Conditions for proceeding**
1. **Written GAM pre-approval** (موافقة مبدئية) of the coffee activity class for the specific unit, plus a zoning check, **before signing a lease**. This is the deal-breaker test.
2. **Volume validated** by footfall counts, a 2-week pop-up and pre-sold 20-drink passes (target: 100 passes in 3 weeks).
3. **Funding of about JOD 44,000–48,000** committed, not JOD 40,000.

**Next steps (about 4–6 weeks, under about JOD 1,500):** shortlist 3 units → zoning certificate and licensing-agent pre-approval for each → footfall counts and pop-up/pass pre-sale → price test at the pop-up → supplier and fit-out quotes → sign a lease only with a licence condition precedent.

## 4. Competitive analysis

**What this means for the decision.** Rivalry is high and entry barriers are low, so the outlet cannot win on price against Qahwa BLK or on ambience against the specialty sit-down cafés. A real gap exists at **JOD 2.25–2.75 for specialty beans served at kiosk speed**, and it is the basis of the whole plan; if the price test fails, the project weakens quickly.

**Landscape.** More than 100 branded coffee outlets in Jordan, led by Starbucks (30) [15]. Local grab-and-go chain Qahwa BLK lists 15+ Amman locations, including University St, Khalda, Sweifieh and Abdoun [13]. Kiosks are cheap to start (used kiosks from JOD 180 [29]).

**Five forces:** rivalry High; new entrants High; buyer power High; supplier power Medium; substitutes Medium–High (Arabic/Turkish coffee at home, RTD concentrates at JOD 8–11 per litre [20]).

**Gaps to exploit:** (1) specialty quality at JOD 2.25–2.75; (2) speed — reviews cite 25–45-minute waits at café-lounges [21]; (3) office batch boxes (Qahwa BLK's 12-cup box at JOD 25 shows demand [16]); (4) batch-brew/cold brew at JOD 2.00–2.50; (5) monthly passes. **Recommended positioning: "Specialty beans, kiosk speed, fair price"**, latte at JOD 2.50.

## 6. Technical and operational study

**What this means for the decision.** The equipment and premises are affordable (capex about JOD 20,900) and capacity is not a constraint: year-3 demand uses about half of practical capacity. The real operational limits are **staffing hours** (see the risk section: two staff cannot legally cover 98 opening hours a week) and the owner's lack of café experience, which makes the head barista a key person.

**Capacity:** about 60 drinks/hour with 2 baristas (35 with one); practical capacity **400 drinks/day** (12,000/month), all ASSUMPTION. Year-1 demand uses about 30% of capacity, year 3 about 53%. The morning peak (07:30–10:00) needs two baristas from year 2.

## 7. Legal and regulatory study

**What this means for the decision.** Taxes and labour rules are moderate and predictable; **licensing is the deciding issue**. GAM refuses new licences for street coffee sales, says a cafeteria licence does not cover coffee, and is actively closing outlets. The project can only proceed at a unit where GAM confirms in writing that prepared coffee is a licensable activity.

**Red flags**
- GAM: no new licences for cafeterias selling coffee to drivers and pedestrians; existing renewals under review [66]. In 2026 GAM counted about **3,700 coffee kiosks**, recorded **309 violations** and closed **145 outlets** [67]. Confidence High that this is the deciding legal issue.
- The 2023 draft hot-beverage regulation (enactment unverified) requires a commercial zone (not local-commercial inside a residential block), at least 100 m from squares, intersections, traffic lights and ramps, no service outside the shop, and a JOD 500 deposit; closures of 3, 7 or 30 days for violations [94][95].
- Zoning: residential-zoned frontage may be refused. Official fee schedules were not retrievable online.

Its value depends on three assumptions that can all be tested for under about JOD 1,500 before any money is committed. This is the case used for the verdict.

## 10. Conclusions, recommendations and next steps

**What this means for the decision.** Proceed, but only through a staged, low-cost validation that answers the licence, volume and price questions before the lease is signed. If any of the three tests fails, stop: the downside of committing first (up to about JOD 22,500 lost on a licence refusal) far exceeds the cost of testing (under about JOD 1,500).

**Conclusions**
1. **Market:** large, young, growing; the outlet needs only a small share. Data quality is the weakness, not demand.
2. **Competition:** crowded, but a clear "specialty at grab-and-go price and speed" gap exists at JOD 2.25–2.75.
3. **Operations:** affordable equipment, ample capacity; staffing hours and the head barista are the operational pinch points.
4. **Legal:** licensing is decisive. Street kiosks are not licensable; a shop unit may be, subject to GAM's written confirmation and zoning.
5. **Financials:** Base returns are very high but optimistic; the Prudent case is positive (NPV JOD 25,803, IRR 31.1%) but thin, with a year-1 loss and payback in month 37.

**Recommendations**
- Plan, fund and judge the project on the **Prudent case**; treat Base as upside.
- Raise **JOD 44,000–48,000**, not 40,000, and keep a standby reserve.
- Hold the **JOD 2.45 ticket**: cap passes and boxes at about 12% of drinks.
- Budget a **third barista** (or commit the owner to about 40 h a week on the bar).
- Form an **LLC** (21% tax, limited liability) once profit is expected above about JOD 20k a year; confirm with a lawyer.
- Consider a **used espresso machine** to protect cash (saves about JOD 1,800).

## Appendix C: Open questions for the owner

**Licensing and legal**
1. Can you shortlist 2–3 actual units, and will their landlords accept a licence condition precedent (rent and key money refunded if GAM refuses within 90 days)?
2. Is the owner a Jordanian national? Foreign ownership triggers investment rules.
3. Sole proprietorship or LLC? The model assumes an LLC (21% tax).
4. Will the baristas be Jordanian? If not, check that the occupation is open to foreign workers.
5. Will you get a written quote from a lawyer or licensing agent for all government fees, which could not be verified online? Has the minimum wage changed in 2026?

**Money**
6. Can you fund JOD 44,000–48,000 rather than 40,000?
7. Do shortlisted units ask for key money or a full year's rent in advance?
8. Will you draw a salary (JOD 600 assumed in Prudent), and will you work the bar about 40 h a week (which would remove the need for a third barista)?
9. What merchant discount rate does your bank offer on card payments (1.5% assumed)?

**Market and operations**
10. Do you have access to a licensed site (office lobby, campus event) for a 2-week pop-up?
11. Will you trade through Ramadan daytime and the university summer break, or shorten hours?
12. Positioning: are you comfortable at a JOD 2.50 latte, and with passes at JOD 1.50–2.00 per drink capped at about 12% of drinks?
13. Which local roaster will be named on the menu, and on what wholesale terms for 10–15 kg/month?
14. New espresso machine with warranty (~JOD 3,150) or used (JOD 1,000–1,500)? Bake-off pastry or daily bakery delivery?
15. Will you list on Talabat/Careem, at the same prices or ~10% higher? Ask both for written commission quotes.
16. Can you name 5–10 offices within 500 m for sampling and office boxes in month 1?
17. Who will run Instagram day to day? A freelancer is not budgeted.
18. Is it worth about USD 1–2k to buy the Euromonitor or World Coffee Portal report to firm up market size?

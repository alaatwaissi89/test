# 01 · Market Study: Specialty Coffee Takeaway Kiosk, Amman

*Prepared by: market-analyst agent · Research date: 15 October 2026 · Currency: JOD (USD converted at the fixed peg, 1 USD = 0.709 JOD)*

> **Bottom line (Assessment):** The market for café coffee in Amman is large, young and growing, so demand is not the constraint. The constraints are competition and positioning. A single kiosk needs only a very small share of its catchment (about 2.5–4.5% over three years) to reach roughly 115–210 cups a day. The free data available leaves the market size uncertain (**Low–Medium confidence**). Before committing, the owner should run a footfall count at the chosen site, which costs little.

---

## 1. Market definition

| Dimension | Definition used |
|---|---|
| Product | Freshly made espresso drinks, cold brew and filter coffee sold **for takeaway** (walk-up plus delivery apps), with bought-in pastries as an add-on. Excludes home coffee (Arabic/Turkish ground coffee, instant) and sit-down café "experience" spend. |
| Customer | B2C: university students, office workers and young professionals. A small B2B add-on is possible (office bulk orders). |
| Geography | **TAM:** Amman Governorate (out-of-home coffee drinks). **SAM:** the kiosk's trading area, meaning a walk-up radius of about 1–1.5 km plus a delivery radius of about 3 km in a mixed office/university district (e.g. around the University of Jordan, Khalda or Sweifieh; the operations agent picks the site). **SOM:** the share this one kiosk can realistically capture. |

### 2d. Reconciliation and TAM / SAM / SOM

The bottom-up figure (JOD 173 m) falls inside the top-down range (JOD 108–215 m) and close to its upper half. **Value used: TAM ≈ JOD 175 m per year** (Low–Medium confidence). The main uncertainty is the share of beans that goes into café drinks; no free source reports it, and the full Euromonitor and Statista figures are behind paywalls [S7][S8].

**SAM (catchment):** ASSUMPTION of about 60,000 target people within the walk-up and delivery radius. This is about 24,000 students if the site is near the University of Jordan (UJ reports a student total of 23,745 [S9]) plus about 36,000 office workers and young residents. The 36,000 figure is a placeholder for the operations agent to replace with site evidence. Because the catchment skews young and employed, the share of regular buyers is assumed to be 35 % rather than 25 %, at 80 drinks per year and JOD 2.75 per drink.
60,000 × 35 % × 80 × 2.75 = **JOD 4.6 m per year**.

**SOM (this kiosk):** These are ASSUMPTION ramp-up shares for a new, independent kiosk next to established chains.

## 3. Growth and trends

- **Coffee spending is growing fast in value.** Coffee import value rose from USD 109.1 m (2021) to USD 171.7 m (2023), a **25.4 % CAGR** [S3]. Much of this is price-driven. Euromonitor (Mar 2026) says rising bean prices lifted value sales in 2025 and expects continued growth from population, tourism and demographics [S7]. **Value used for the café segment: 6 % nominal CAGR.** This is an ASSUMPTION built from population growth of 1.7 % [S1], price increases of about 2–3 % and some premiumisation. It deliberately leaves out the import spike (Low confidence).
- **Young population.** The median age is 22.9 and 44.7 % of people are under 20 [S10]. FNF (Dec 2025) describes a youth-driven "café boom" in Amman, with specialty-coffee momentum since about 2015 [S11].
- **Café culture is rising in foodservice.** Euromonitor reports that café popularity and more socialising boosted foodservice hot-drink sales in 2025 [S7].
- **Consumers favour local brands.** Since October 2023 a boycott has hit US chains. NPR reported a near-empty Starbucks in Amman, while the local chain Astrolabe reported business up 30–40 % (founder's own estimate) [S12]. Assessment: this favours a local, independent brand.
- **Delivery.** Careem promotes local coffee brands for delivery in Jordan [S13], and Talabat lists coffee categories [S14]. No public order-volume data was found.
- **Seasonality (Assessment, no sourced figures):** daytime sales are likely to drop during Ramadan, with evenings stronger; cold brew lifts sales in summer; student traffic follows the university calendar, so sales near campus will fall in summer.

## 4. Customer segments

| Segment | Size in catchment | Needs | Willingness to pay (per drink) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **University students** | ~24,000 if near UJ (UJ student total 23,745, 26 % international [S9]; UJ's own figures may be higher, so verify) | Low price, speed between classes, iced drinks, student deals, delivery to campus gates | JOD 1.75–2.50. ASSUMPTION anchored on the low end of the JOD 2.00–4.00 range [S5] | Medium |
| **Office workers** | ASSUMPTION ~20,000 (depends on site) | Fast morning service, pre-ordering, consistency, bulk office orders | JOD 2.50–3.25 [S5][S6] | Low |
| **Young professionals and residents** | ASSUMPTION ~16,000 | Specialty quality, single origin, cold brew, a brand to identify with, evening and weekend visits | JOD 3.00–4.00 (top of the Numbeo range [S5]) | Low |
| **Delivery-app customers** (overlaps the groups above) | Not quantified | Convenience; price includes app commission | Menu price + fee | Low |

Affordability: one JOD 2.75 drink is about 0.5 % of the average monthly net salary of JOD 532 [S5]. A habit of 80 drinks a year costs about JOD 220, roughly 3.4 % of annual net salary. **Assessment:** this is fine for professionals but significant for students, so the kiosk needs a two-tier menu (a value drink plus a specialty range).

## 5. Demand evidence

- **The branded café market is established and growing.** It had more than 100 branded outlets (2023), led by Starbucks (30), with Dimitri's (8, all in Amman), Coffee Bean & Tea Leaf (6) and Costa (5). % Arabica opened at Abdali, Amman in 2024 [S15].
- **Local chains are scaling.** Qahwa BLK has 20+ branches across Jordan, and several other independent brands are active on Careem [S13]. Astrolabe has 7 Amman branches and is expanding abroad [S12].
- **Consumption volume.** Green coffee imports rose 34 % in 2023, to 33.5 m kg [S4].
- **Prices are observable and stable.** Two independent sources put the cappuccino price at JOD 2.60–3.16 [S5][S6].
- **Gap:** no site-level evidence (footfall, competitor queue counts, Google Maps review volumes) has been collected yet. That is the operations agent's task.

## 6. Assessment

**Market attractiveness: 3.5 / 5** (Assessment)

- (+) A large, young, urban customer base, with café habits growing and coffee spending rising in value.
- (+) Local and independent brands are favoured (boycott effect), and delivery apps lower the barrier to reaching customers.
- (+) The kiosk needs only a very small share of its catchment.
- (−) The market is crowded: chains plus fast-growing local brands, with many outlets in West Amman.
- (−) Students are price-sensitive and their demand is seasonal.
- (−) Market-size data is weak. The paid reports were not accessed, and the café share of beans is an assumption.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Which site?** The catchment (60,000 people) is a placeholder. A 2-day footfall count (7–10 am and 1–4 pm) at 2–3 candidate sites would turn the SAM and SOM from Low to Medium confidence.
2. **Positioning:** a value drink for students (about JOD 1.75–2.00) or a premium specialty range (JOD 3–4)? This choice changes the average spend and the segment mix.
3. **Delivery apps:** are you willing to list on Careem and Talabat and accept their commission? What share of sales do you expect from delivery?
4. **Paid data:** is it worth about USD 1–2k to buy the Euromonitor "Hot Drinks in Jordan" or World Coffee Portal "Project Café" report and firm up the TAM and CAGR?
5. **Operating calendar:** will you open during Ramadan daytime and through the university summer break? This affects the operating-days assumption (360).

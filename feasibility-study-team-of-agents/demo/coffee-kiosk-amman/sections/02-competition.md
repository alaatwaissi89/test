# 02 — Competitive Analysis: Specialty Coffee Takeaway Kiosk, Amman

*Prepared by: competitor-analyst agent · Research date: 15 Oct 2026 · Currency: JOD*

All menu prices below come from the public Talabat Jordan menus, fetched on **15 Oct 2026**. Delivery-app prices can be slightly higher than walk-up prices, and Talabat shows "Price on Selection" for drinks sold in several sizes, so many lattes at Dimitri's, Costa, Coffee Factory and % Arabica have no single listed price. Where a price could not be seen it is marked "n/s" (not shown) and **not estimated**.

---

## 1. Competitive landscape

**The market is crowded and still growing.**
- World Coffee Portal (March 2024) counts **more than 100 branded coffee-shop outlets in Jordan**, with international chains holding a large share. Starbucks leads with **30 stores**. The Coffee Bean & Tea Leaf has 6 and Costa Coffee has 5. Among local chains, Dimitri's Coffee and Donuts Factory each have 8 outlets in Amman [S1]. *Confidence: Medium (2024 data, before % Arabica and the recent growth of Qahwa BLK).*
- Since then, % Arabica has opened at Abdali Boulevard (March 2024) [S1]. Local grab-and-go chain **Qahwa BLK** now lists **15+ Amman locations, including University Street, Khalda, Sweifieh and Abdoun** [S2]. Jordan News (May 2025) says the local coffee scene has "grown over the past two years" and that independents are taking share [S2].
- On demand, the Friedrich Naumann Foundation (Dec 2025) notes that specialty coffee in Amman took off around 2015. It describes cafés as a core social and work space for young people, with "half the country under the age of 24" [S3]. *Confidence: Medium (qualitative).*

**Three competitive tiers matter for a 15–25 m² takeaway kiosk:**

| Tier | Who | Typical milk-espresso price | Relevance to kiosk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Value grab-and-go | Qahwa BLK, informal street kiosks, subscription americano offers | JOD 1.00–2.50 | **Most direct threat.** Same format, same customers (students, office walk-ups). |
| B. International chains | Starbucks, Costa, Coffee Bean | JOD 2.60–4.20 | Brand pull, sites in malls and on main streets; reviews mention high price and slow service |
| C. Local specialty / third-wave | Dimitri's, % Arabica, Almond, Astrolabe, Coffee Factory, Kofy Haus, Base | JOD 3.00–5.00+ (brewed, signature drinks) | Set the quality standard; mostly sit-down cafés, not kiosks |

**Indirect competitors and substitutes:** Turkish/Arabic coffee from roasteries and home brewing (still the dominant home habit [S3]), bakeries with coffee (Crumz, PAUL), supermarket instant and iced coffee, and RTD concentrates (Dimitri's sells a 1 L iced-latte concentrate for JOD 8.00–11.00 [S8]).

## 4. Porter's five forces

| Force | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry among existing competitors | **High** | 100+ branded outlets [S1]. Qahwa BLK alone has 15+ grab-and-go sites, including University St and Khalda [S2]. Coffee Factory runs 20% app discounts (e.g. frappe 5.00 to 4.00) [S16]. A JOD 1.00/cup subscription offer exists [S18]. |
| Threat of new entrants | **High** | Kiosks are cheap to start (used kiosks advertised from JOD 180 [S17]). The concept is easy to copy, and new local brands keep appearing [S2]. The barriers are site access and quality, not capital. |
| Bargaining power of buyers | **High** | Switching costs are zero, many alternatives exist within walking distance, and delivery apps allow side-by-side price comparison. Students are price-sensitive (Assessment). |
| Bargaining power of suppliers | **Medium** | Several local roasters exist (Dimitri's also sells beans: 250 g from JOD 5.78–8.25 [S8]). Imported green coffee follows world prices, and machines and spare parts are imported (Assessment; see the cost section). |
| Threat of substitutes | **Medium–High** | Arabic/Turkish coffee at home and from roasteries is the dominant habit [S3]. RTD concentrates (1 L for JOD 8–11 [S8]) and supermarket coffee are alternatives. |

**Barriers to entry (for us).** Securing a high-footfall kiosk site in the target area against chains. Building quality consistency without café experience (the brief says the owner has none). Getting visible on Talabat and Careem, where established brands dominate the "Picks" slots.

## 5. Gaps and opportunities

1. **The "specialty quality at grab-and-go price" gap.** Qahwa BLK owns the JOD 2.00 latte but sells on price and speed, not bean quality. Specialty brands (Dimitri's, Arabica, Almond) charge JOD 3.00+ and are sit-down formats. There is space at **JOD 2.25–2.75** for a kiosk that names its single-origin beans. *Assessment, Medium confidence.*
2. **Speed of service.** The most common complaint across Almond, Coffee Bean, Books@Cafe and Cafe Kepi is slow service (25–45 minutes in cited reviews) [S9]. A kiosk built for under-3-minute service fixes the main pain point of the café-lounge model.
3. **Seating and noise fatigue.** Reviews mention "crowded and loud", smoking areas, and hard parking [S9]. A walk-up or drive-by kiosk with short-stop parking helps office workers who want coffee without a café visit.
4. **Office B2B batch orders.** Qahwa BLK sells a 12-person coffee + fatayer box at JOD 25 [S4], which shows office demand. Few specialty brands advertise this. It is an opportunity in Sweifieh/Khalda office clusters.
5. **Brewed and cold-brew at an accessible price.** Filter coffee is JOD 3.00–3.74 at specialty cafés [S10][S14]. A JOD 2.00–2.50 batch-brew or cold-brew would be unique in the grab-and-go tier.
6. **Subscriptions and loyalty.** One operator sells 30 americanos for JOD 30 [S18]. A student/office monthly pass is a proven local hook for repeat visits.

**Underserved areas (Assessment, Low confidence; needs a site walk).** University Street already has Starbucks, Costa and Qahwa BLK [S5][S7][S13]. Khalda has Qahwa BLK and Astrolabe [S2]. Abdoun is the most saturated (Starbucks, BLK, Coffee Factory, Base, Comma, Majnoon) [S2][S9]. Pockets of Khalda or Sweifieh office streets look less saturated than Abdoun or University St. The operations agent should verify with Google Maps counts within 500 m.

## 6. Recommended positioning

**"Specialty beans, kiosk speed, fair price."**
- **Price:** core latte at **JOD 2.50** (between Qahwa BLK at 2.00 and Starbucks at 2.90 or Dimitri's at 3.00). Americano at JOD 1.75, Spanish latte at JOD 3.00, cold brew at JOD 2.75. *ASSUMPTION, derived from the benchmark midpoints; the finance agent should stress-test this at JOD 2.00 and 3.00.*
- **Proof points:** named local roaster and origin on the menu, a 3-minute service promise, and a mobile pre-order or Talabat pickup option.
- **Hooks:** a student and office monthly pass (e.g. 20 drinks), and office batch boxes.
- **Avoid:** competing with Qahwa BLK on price alone (it has scale), and competing with Dimitri's/Arabica on ambience (a kiosk has no seating).

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Exact site.** Which street or building? Competition density differs sharply between Abdoun (saturated) and Khalda/Sweifieh office pockets. A 500 m competitor count and a peak-hour footfall count are needed.
2. **Positioning appetite.** Are you comfortable at a JOD 2.50 latte (above Qahwa BLK, below Starbucks), or do you prefer a volume play at JOD 2.00?
3. **Bean supplier.** Will you buy from a local roaster (Dimitri's and others sell wholesale/retail) or import? Naming the roaster is part of the positioning.
4. **Delivery apps.** Do you accept Talabat/Careem commissions (rate to be confirmed by the finance agent), or will you focus on walk-up and pickup only?
5. **B2B.** Do you have office contacts in the chosen area for batch-box and subscription sales?
6. **Price verification.** Should we do a one-day walk-up price check at Dimitri's, Costa, Arabica and Coffee Factory to fill the "n/s" gaps that the delivery apps hide?

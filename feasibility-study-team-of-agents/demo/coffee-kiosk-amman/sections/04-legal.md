# 04 · Legal & Regulatory Study: Specialty Coffee Kiosk, Amman

> This section is desk research, not legal advice. Before signing a lease or registering anything, confirm every item with a Jordanian lawyer and a licensed accountant. Research date: 15 Oct 2026.

## Summary

- **The biggest risk is regulatory, not cost.** Greater Amman Municipality (GAM) says it will issue **no new licences to cafeterias that sell coffee to drivers and pedestrians on the street**. Its position is that a cafeteria licence covers "sandwiches and beverages", not coffee, and it is reviewing existing renewals ([GAM media centre](https://media.ammancity.gov.jo/En/NewsDetails/GAM__No_New_licenses_for_cafeterias_Selling_Coffee_for_Drivers_and_Pedestrians)). In 2026 GAM counted about **3,700 coffee kiosks**, recorded **309 violations** and closed **145 outlets** ([Jordan News, 30 Aug 2026](https://www.jordannews.jo/Section-109/News/Greater-Amman-Municipality-Nearly-3-700-Coffee-Kiosks-in-the-Capital-309-Violations-Recorded-Since-Early-2026-54908)). Confidence: **High** that this is the deciding legal issue.
- Taxes are simple and moderate: **20% corporate income tax + 1% national contribution** and **16% general sales tax** ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/jordan/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)).
- Labour costs: minimum wage is **JOD 290/month**, and the employer pays **14.25% social security** on top ([WageIndicator](https://wageindicator.org/salary/minimum-wage/minimum-wages-news/2025/minimum-wage-updated-in-jordan-from-01-january-2025-january-15-2025); [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/jordan/corporate/other-taxes)).

## Legal form

| Option | Minimum capital | Liability | Fit for this project | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual establishment (sole proprietorship), registered with the Ministry of Industry, Trade & Supply (MoITS) | No fixed minimum found | Unlimited (owner's personal assets) | Simplest and cheapest. Profit is taxed at personal rates of 5–25%. | [Jordan e-Gov service listing](https://form.jordan.gov.jo/wps/portal/Home/GovernmentEntities/Ministries/MinistryServiceDetails_en/ministry%20of%20industry%20and%20trade/services/individual%20institutes%20registry?lang=en) (page blocked to automated fetch; fees not retrieved) |
| Limited Liability Company (LLC), registered with the Companies Control Department (CCD) | **JOD 1**, per the Companies Law amendment | Limited to capital | Recommended if the owner wants to protect personal assets or add a partner | [Al Tamimi, 2017](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update/february-9/articles/the-endless-puzzling-question-what-type-of-company-should-i-set-up-in-jordan/); [Enterprise Singapore, 2022](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/grow-your-business/go-global/market-guides/middle-east/jordan/business-guide) |
| Private shareholding company (PSC) | JOD 50,000 | Limited | Not suitable: needs more capital than the JOD 40k budget | [Al Tamimi, 2017](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update/february-9/articles/the-endless-puzzling-question-what-type-of-company-should-i-set-up-in-jordan/) |

The sources conflict on the LLC minimum. One consultancy says the LLC minimum is JOD 1,000 and that an LLC needs two or more shareholders ([Expanship](https://www.expanship.com/jo/blog/incorporation-requirements-in-jordan)). The same page contradicts itself on whether a sole shareholder is allowed. Confidence on LLC rules: **Medium**. Confirm with the CCD.

**Assessment:** If the owner is a Jordanian national, use either an individual establishment (fastest) or a single-owner LLC. Under personal rates, profit above JOD 20k is taxed at 25%, which is higher than the 21% company rate. So once profits pass about JOD 20k a year, an LLC becomes tax-efficient as well as protective.

**Total government setup fees: not verifiable from official sources online.** Working figure: **ASSUMPTION JOD 1,500** for all registrations, licences and certificates, plus about **JOD 500–1,000** for a lawyer or accountant. Confidence: **Low**. The owner should get a written quote from a local licensing agent (معقب معاملات) or lawyer.

**Time to licence: ASSUMPTION 2–3 months.** Steps 1 and 2 must come before step 3, and GAM's inspection backlog and the current review of coffee outlets may add delay. This still fits the brief's 4–6 month opening target only if the site is already zoned for commercial use. Confidence: **Low–Medium**.

**Assessment:** A busy kiosk will almost certainly pass the JOD 30k or 75k threshold, so the financial model should treat menu prices as **GST-inclusive at 16%**. No sector incentive or free zone applies to an Amman retail kiosk.

**Employer cost of one minimum-wage worker:** 290 × 1.1425 = **JOD 331/month**. An experienced barista will earn above minimum wage; the market or operations agent should provide that figure.

Two hiring cautions:
- **Opening hours:** a kiosk open around 14 hours a day needs at least 2 shifts to stay within 48 hours per person per week.
- **Foreign workers:** they need a Ministry of Labour work permit, and some service occupations are closed to non-Jordanians ([Enterprise Singapore](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/grow-your-business/go-global/market-guides/middle-east/jordan/business-guide)). The barista occupation list was not verified; this is an open question.

## Compliance

- **Food safety:** Food Law No. 30/2015 applies. The JFDA can inspect at any time and closes non-compliant outlets ([ARDD](https://ardd-jo.org/?p=35310)). Plan for running water, a handwash sink, cold-chain storage for milk and pastries, and supplier invoices that trace the pastries' origin.
- **GAM hot-beverage rules (2026):** GAM has started enforcing regulations on preparing and selling hot drinks, with fines. Selling into the road or approaching cars leads to notice, then a violation, then closure ([Jordan News, Aug 2026](https://www.jordannews.jo/Section-109/News/Greater-Amman-Municipality-Nearly-3-700-Coffee-Kiosks-in-the-Capital-309-Violations-Recorded-Since-Early-2026-54908)).
- **E-invoicing:** the POS must issue JoFotara-compliant invoices.
- **Insurance:** we found no statutory requirement beyond SSC work-injury cover. Assessment: buy fire and third-party liability cover; landlords usually require it.
- **Delivery apps:** delivery staff need health certificates, and the platforms may ask for the vocational licence before onboarding the kiosk.

## Red flags

1. **Licence class (critical).** GAM states that a cafeteria licence does **not** cover coffee, and it refuses new licences for street coffee sales aimed at drivers and pedestrians ([GAM](https://media.ammancity.gov.jo/En/NewsDetails/GAM__No_New_licenses_for_cafeterias_Selling_Coffee_for_Drivers_and_Pedestrians)). A walk-up kiosk on the street front could fall into this category.
   - **Mitigation:** before signing a lease, get GAM's written confirmation of the exact profession classification for selling prepared coffee (for example, a café or coffee-shop activity rather than a cafeteria) at that specific address.
   - Prefer a unit **inside** a mall, office lobby or campus, or one with its own forecourt and no kerbside service.
2. **Enforcement climate.** 145 closures in 2026 show active enforcement. Any business model built on serving cars is high-risk.
3. **Zoning.** The plot's land use must permit commercial activity. Residential-zoned frontage in Abdoun or Khalda may be refused.
4. **Sales-tax rate.** The 16% GST is fixed unless the site is tourist-classified, which is unlikely for a kiosk.
5. **Fee opacity.** Official fee schedules were not retrievable online, so budget a contingency.

No local-partner requirement applies to a Jordanian owner. A foreign owner would need to check the investment rules for foreign ownership of food services (open question).

## Open questions for the owner

1. Is the owner a Jordanian national? Foreign ownership triggers investment-registration rules and possible restrictions.
2. Is the target unit inside a building, mall or campus, or on the street front with walk-up service? This decides whether GAM will license it.
3. Has GAM confirmed in writing which activity class covers prepared coffee at the shortlisted address, and is the plot zoned commercial?
4. Sole proprietorship or LLC? This depends on the owner's appetite for personal liability and on expected profit.
5. Will the barista be Jordanian? If not, check that the occupation is open to foreign workers and budget for permit fees.
6. Has the minimum wage changed in 2026? The figure above is the 1 Jan 2025 rate; confirm with the Ministry of Labour.
7. Get a written quote from a local lawyer or licensing agent for all government fees, which could not be verified online.
